GEMB.apply_blowing_snow! Method
julia
apply_blowing_snow!(cols, cfs, mp) -> (mass_sublimated, E_sublimated)

Compact and fragment near-surface snow under wind, and optionally sublimate the suspended fraction. The Crocus SNOWDRIFT scheme (snowcro.F90:4685-4950; Lafaysse et al., 2026 eqs. 59-66), ported for GEMB's state variables.

Per layer, from the surface down:

  1. Mobility (mobility_index) and driftability (driftability_index) from the layer's own microstructure, density, and the 5 m gust speed.

  2. Stop at the first layer that will not drift. The overburden a layer sits under can only grow with depth, so once driftability reaches zero it stays zero. This is the source's EXIT at snowcro.F90:4844; the paper's eq. 63 does not show it, and a literal port of the equation would walk the whole column computing zeros.

  3. The effective driftability DEFF (eq. 63): the layer's own driftability decayed by exp(-100·Σ 0.5·dz_j·0.1·(3.25 - D_j)) over the overburden, counted a half-layer at a time so that each layer is decayed by the half of itself above its own centre. Deeper snow is reworked less, and snow under mobile snow (large D_j) is decayed less than snow under a crust — the overburden's own driftability enters the exponent.

  4. Compaction toward DRIFT_DENSITY_MAX (eq. 64) at the rate DEFF·dt/τ_DRIFT, exactly conservative in mass: dz is rescaled by the density ratio. Snow already at or above 350 kg m-3 is left alone, which is the source's IF (PSNOWRHO < XVROMAX) gate.

  5. Grain fragmentation (eqs. 65-66): dendricity falls, sphericity rises toward 1, and grain size falls toward DRIFT_GRAIN_SIZE_MIN — saltating grains break up and round off. This is the mechanism by which drifted snow is optically and mechanically distinct from the snow it came from, not merely denser.

When mp.blowing_snow_sublimation is set, the surface layer also loses mass at blowing_snow_sublimation_rate, capped at half the layer's thickness per step (the source's MAX(0.5*PSNOWDZ, ...), which keeps a long step from emptying the layer), and that rate feeds back into the compaction rate through the ZQS_EFFECT term: a vigorously sublimating surface is also being vigorously reworked.

Returns the sublimated (mass, energy), both zero unless mp.blowing_snow_sublimation — the compaction and fragmentation path moves no mass and carries no energy across the surface, so it contributes nothing to either budget. Both are negative, signed as a flux into the column the way apply_horizontal_strain!'s are, so the caller adds rather than subtracts them.

The latent heat is not charged to the column

The energy returned is the departing snow's own enthalpy only — no LS. Blowing-snow sublimation happens to snow already airborne, in the saltation and suspension layers, so the latent heat is drawn from the air the particles are suspended in, not from the pack: that cooling and moistening of the near-surface air is precisely the effect Gordon et al. (2006) added the parameterization to capture. Crocus is consistent with this — SNOWDRIFT writes PSNOWDZ and reports the flux in PSNDRIFT, but applies no temperature or enthalpy tendency to the layer it took the mass from.

The distinction is against surface sublimation, which GEMB does charge LS to the column (through heat_flux_latent in calculate_temperature) because that phase change happens at the ice-air interface. Charging LS here as well would double-count it: with a 2 h step and a windy forcing it fails the verbose energy check by order 1e5 J m-2 per step.

The consequence is that GEMB, like Crocus, does not close the atmospheric energy budget for this term. A coupled model would need the flux back; flux.mass_blowing_snow and the blowing_snow output layer are what it would read.

Returns (0.0, 0.0) and touches nothing when mp.blowing_snow_method === :none, which is what keeps the default bit-identical to a run without the term. Under mp.blowing_snow_method === :Keenan2021 it runs apply_redeposition! instead of the compaction above; the two are not combined, since both represent the densification of wind-transported snow.

GEMB.apply_prescribed_drift! Method
julia
apply_prescribed_drift!(cols, cfs, mp) -> (mass_drift, E_drift)

Apply a prescribed net drift divergence to the surface cell, and return the mass and enthalpy it moved.

This is the IMAU-FDM treatment (source/firn_physics.f90:81-88, Update_Surface), which takes drift as forcing computed upstream by RACMO (Lenaerts et al., 2012) rather than computing it: the flux is a mass sink or source at the surface, with a sign-dependent density, because erosion removes the snow that is actually there while deposition brings snow of whatever density fresh drifted snow has:

  • Erosion (positive flux) removes mass at the surface cell's own density[1], thinning it and taking its pore water in proportion — the cell's density, temperature, and grain properties are unchanged, exactly as sublimation of a fraction of a cell would leave them. Age-neutral for the same reason (see dilute_age).

  • Deposition (negative flux) adds mass at fresh_snow_density — the same density fresh snowfall arrives at under mp.new_snow_method — mixing it into the surface cell. Age-zero mass, so it dilutes age[1], and it arrives at the air temperature, so it mixes the cell's temperature the way snowfall does.

Deposition merges into the surface cell rather than creating one, unlike snowfall: a drift flux is a slow net divergence, not a discrete event, so at any realistic rate the mass added per step is far below mp.column_dzmin and a fresh cell would be merged straight back by manage_layer_thickness. Erosion never removes more than half the surface cell in one step, the same cap apply_blowing_snow! applies to sublimation and for the same reason.

The rate is the optional snow_drift forcing layer when the forcing carries one, and mp.drift_rate (a constant [kg m-2 yr-1], positive for erosion) otherwise. Returns the mass signed as a loss from the column — negative for erosion, positive for deposition — so the caller adds it to the mass budget the way it adds trim_bottom!'s basal flux, and the matching enthalpy.

Unlike apply_blowing_snow!'s sublimation this is a transport term: the mass leaves or arrives as snow, not vapour, so it carries only its own enthalpy and no latent heat.

Returns (0.0, 0.0) and touches nothing when the rate is zero, which is what keeps the drift_rate = 0.0 default bit-identical to a run without the term.

GEMB.apply_redeposition! Method
julia
apply_redeposition!(cols, cfs, mp) -> (0.0, 0.0)

Wind packing of near-surface snow by erosion and redeposition, the SNOWPACK scheme of Keenan et al. (2021) eqs. 3-4, pp. 1067-1068, with the erosion threshold taken from GEMB's Crocus mobility index. Selected by mp.blowing_snow_method = :Keenan2021.

Per step:

  1. The 10 m wind U₁₀ (wind_speed_10m) and the 5 m gust speed U₅ (drift_wind_speed), both over the roughness of the surface cell.

  2. Erosion starts only if U₅ exceeds the threshold U₅,t of the surface cell (redeposition_threshold_wind_speed of its mobility_index) and the surface cell holds no liquid water.

  3. The friction velocity u* = κ·U₁₀/log(10/0.002) (saltation_friction_velocity) and its threshold u*_t = u*·U₅,t/U₅, the friction velocity at which U₅ would equal the threshold.

  4. The eroded mass M = Φ·dt, with Φ the saltation flux of eq. 3 (saltation_mass_flux) at the air density of the forcing and fetch length mp.drift_fetch_length.

  5. M is taken from the top of the column down. Erosion stops at the first cell that is wet or whose own threshold is at or above U₅, or when M is used up. Of a cell of mass m, the reworked mass m_r = min(M, m) is redeposited in the same cell at ρ_rd = 361·log10(U₁₀) + 33 (redeposition_density, eq. 4), so the cell becomes dz = (m − m_r)/ρ + m_r/ρ_rd, ρ = m/dz. A cell already at or above ρ_rd keeps its density: redeposition never lowers it.

  6. In a dendritic cell the reworked fraction f = m_r/m takes the microstructure of new snow above 5 m s-1 in the SNOWPACK "Redeposit" setup, dendricity 0.325 and sphericity 0.875 (Wever et al., 2023, Table 1, p. 827), mixed by mass and capped so that neither moves away from that state: d ← (1−f)·d + f·min(d, 0.325), s ← (1−f)·s + f·max(s, 0.875), with the radius re-derived by dendritic_grain_radius (kept where independent_grain_radius holds). Non-dendritic cells keep their grains.

Mass and energy. The eroded mass is redeposited in the column (Keenan et al. p. 1068: the mass flux "is redeposited at the snow surface"; Wever et al. p. 827: deposition "is equal to the calculated eroded mass"), so nothing crosses the column boundary. Each cell keeps its own mass, temperature, water and age, so column mass and enthalpy are unchanged to rounding, and the function returns (0.0, 0.0) for the blowing-snow budget terms. The grid is not touched beyond the dz of the reworked cells; the grid controllers that run later in the step restore the thickness bands.

Departures from SNOWPACK as Keenan et al. describe it:

  • Threshold. SNOWPACK computes u*_t from Lehning and Fierz (2008), Keenan et al. eq. 2, which needs a bond radius and a coordination number. GEMB carries neither, so the threshold comes from the Crocus mobility index, which is GEMB's own measure of how readily a layer drifts given its dendricity, sphericity, grain size and density (Vionnet et al., 2012). For fresh dendritic snow (d = 1, s = 0.5, ρ = 180 kg m-3) it gives U₅,t = 5.47 m s-1, about 4.7 m s-1 at 10 m.

  • Placement. SNOWPACK removes the eroded elements and deposits a new layer at the surface. Here the reworked mass stays in the cell it came from. The mass and its redeposited density are the same; the vertical order within the eroded depth and the cell count are kept, so the scheme creates and removes no cells.

  • Stability and air density. u* is the neutral value, and ρ_a is the air density of the forcing step where Keenan et al. quote 1.1 kg m-3 (p. 1067, for eq. 2).

  • Wet snow does not erode.

Throws an ArgumentError for a negative or non-finite wind speed.

GEMB.blowing_snow_sublimation_rate Method
julia
blowing_snow_sublimation_rate(mobility, wind_5m, cfs) -> Q_S [kg m-2 s-1]

Sublimation rate of suspended blowing snow, Gordon et al. (2006) as Crocus implements it (snowcro.F90:4851-4859; Lafaysse et al., 2026 eq. 67):

julia
Q_S = a·(T₀/T_a)^4·U_t·ρ_a·q_sat(T_a,P_s)·(1 - q_a/q_sat)·(U₅/U_t)^3.6

Suspended particles have a far larger surface-to-mass ratio than the snow surface, so they sublimate much faster than the surface itself; the rate is driven by the saturation deficit (1 - RH_i) and rises steeply with wind through the ratio to the threshold speed U_t (drift_threshold_wind_speed).

Humidity is with respect to ice, via saturation_vapor_pressure_ice and specific_humidity — the deficit that matters is the one against the sublimating solid, and using the liquid saturation instead would understate it below freezing.

Can be negative (the caller clamps at zero) when the air is supersaturated with respect to ice, which is common in polar winter; the scheme does not represent the deposition that physically corresponds to that.

See DRIFT_SUBLIM_EXP_TEMPERATURE for the paper-versus-source disagreement over which of the two exponents goes on which ratio, and why the source's ordering is used.

GEMB.drift_density_factor Method
julia
drift_density_factor(density) -> F(ρ) [-]

Density term of the mobility index, Lafaysse et al. (2026) eq. 60:

julia
F(ρ) = 1.25 - 0.004·(max(ρ_min, ρ) - ρ_min),   ρ_min = 50 kg m-3

Denser snow is harder to mobilize; the term is flat below DRIFT_DENSITY_MIN and goes negative above ~360 kg m-3, which is what stops a wind slab from being re-eroded indefinitely.

The source writes the slope as 1.25/(1000·XVMOB1) (snowcro.F90:4808), which is 0.0042373, not the 0.004 the paper rounds it to; the source form is used here.

GEMB.drift_threshold_wind_speed Method
julia
drift_threshold_wind_speed(mobility) -> Ut [m s-1]

The 5 m wind speed at which a layer of the given mobility just begins to drift, Lafaysse et al. (2026) eq. 68 — driftability_index solved for D = 0:

julia
U_t = -log((MOB + 1)/2.868)/0.085

Read only by blowing_snow_sublimation_rate, where it sets both the scale of the sublimation rate and the wind ratio it is driven by. This is the "modification of a threshold wind speed U_t to account for the microstructure-related mobility index" the paper describes: Gordon et al. (2006) used a fixed threshold, Crocus makes it a function of the snow's own state.

Undefined for MOB ≥ 1.868 (the log's argument exceeds 1, giving a negative threshold) and divergent as MOB → -1; neither is reachable from the mobility formula at a physical density, and the caller gates on a positive driftability, which implies a threshold below the current wind.

GEMB.drift_wind_speed Method
julia
drift_wind_speed(cfs, z0) -> U5 [m s-1]

Gust speed at DRIFT_WIND_HEIGHT (5 m) above the snow surface, which is the wind the driftability index is defined against.

cfs.wind_speed is given at cfs.wind_observation_height, so it is extrapolated to 5 m through a neutral logarithmic profile over roughness z0 and multiplied by the DRIFT_GUST_COEF gust factor:

julia
U₅ = 1.25·U·log(5/z₀)/log(z_u/z₀)

This is snowcro.F90:4801-4802 exactly, including the three-way floor on the roughness length, z₀ = min(z0, z_u/2, 2.5). The first two bounds keep the two logs finite and same-signed when the observation height is low; the 2.5 m bound (half the 5 m reference height, PPHREF_MIN in the source) keeps a very rough surface from inverting the ratio. Vionnet added the systematic use of a 5 m wind in 2014 precisely so the index does not depend on the forcing's observation height.

Note the gust factor is removed again wherever a rate is formed from U₅ (.../DRIFT_GUST_COEF in apply_blowing_snow! and blowing_snow_sublimation_rate), matching XCOEF_FF appearing in both places in the source: the gust speed sets whether and how vigorously snow moves, but the mass flux is scaled back to the mean wind.

z0 comes from surface_roughness, so the drift scheme and the turbulent fluxes see the same surface.

GEMB.driftability_index Method
julia
driftability_index(mobility, wind_5m) -> D [-]

Driftability of one layer from its mobility and the 5 m gust speed, Lafaysse et al. (2026) eq. 62:

julia
D = max(MOB - (2.868·exp(-0.085·U₅) - 1), 0)

The bracket is the mobility a layer must have for the given wind to move it: it falls from 1.868 at zero wind to 0 at U₅ = log(2.868)/0.085 = 12.4 m s-1, above which every layer with non-negative mobility drifts. Inverting it gives the threshold wind speed drift_threshold_wind_speed that the sublimation rate is written against.

The max(·, 0) is in the paper but not in the source, which instead exits its layer loop at the first non-positive value — see apply_blowing_snow!, which follows the source. Both are kept: the clamp here makes the function well-defined for a caller asking about a single layer.

GEMB.mobility_index Method
julia
mobility_index(grain_radius, dendricity, sphericity, density, wet) -> MOB [-]

Mobility index of one layer, Guyomarc'h & Mérindol (1998) as Crocus's B92 branch implements it (snowcro.F90:4812-4820; Lafaysse et al., 2026 eq. 59, SNOWMOB = GM98 line combined with the 0.34/0.66 weighting):

julia
dendritic:      MOB = 0.34·(0.5 - (0.75δ' + 0.5S')/99) + 0.66·F(ρ)
non-dendritic:  MOB = 0.34·(0.833 - 0.833·S'/99 - 0.583·gs) + 0.66·F(ρ)

Fresh dendritic crystals and small rounded grains are the most mobile; large faceted grains and dense snow the least. F is drift_density_factor.

Crocus stores dendricity and sphericity as the Brun et al. (1992) coded integers, negative dendricity in PSNOWGRAN1 and sphericity on 0-99 in PSNOWGRAN2, where GEMB carries both as fractions on [0, 1]; the /99 and sign conventions of the source are undone here so the formula reads in GEMB's units. gs is the grain size (diameter) in metres, which is 2·grain_radius·1e-3.

wet caps the index at DRIFT_MOB4 (eq. 61): snow that has held liquid water is bonded and resists drift regardless of its grain shape.

Deviation from Crocus: no wet-snow history

Crocus applies that cap on PSNOWHIST ≥ 2, i.e. whether the layer has held liquid water at any time in its history. GEMB's column state has no such field, and adding one would extend RESTART_LAYERS and break the restart contract, so the caller passes the instantaneous water[i] > WATER_TOLERANCE instead. The consequence is one-directional: a refrozen layer that is currently dry is not capped here, so it is more driftable than Crocus would make it, and a melt-affected surface can be wind-packed a second time. If this proves to matter, a wet_history layer field is a separate, restart-breaking change.

GEMB.saltation_mass_flux Method
julia
saltation_mass_flux(density_air, u_star, u_star_threshold, fetch_length) -> Φ [kg m-2 s-1]

Saltation mass flux eroded from the snow surface, Keenan et al. (2021) eq. 3, p. 1067:

julia
Φ = 0.0014·ρ_a·u*·(u* − u*_t)·(u* + 7.6·u*_t + 205)/L

The saltation transport rate Q [kg m-1 s-1] of Lehning and Fierz (2008) divided by the fetch length L [m]. Zero when u* ≤ u*_t. The additive 205 is as printed in Keenan et al. and as SNOWPACK evaluates it; Wever et al. (2023, pp. 827-828) note that the coefficients of this expression carry inconsistent units, and that the simulations agree with observations regardless. It dominates the last factor, so in practice Φ ≈ 0.287·ρ_a·u*·(u* − u*_t)/L.

Throws an ArgumentError for a non-positive fetch length or a negative friction velocity.

GEMB.calculate_accumulation Method
julia
calculate_accumulation(temperature, dz, density, water, grain_radius, grain_dendricity, grain_sphericity, age, cfs::ClimateForcingStep, mp::ModelParameters, verbose::Bool)

Add precipitation and deposition to the model column.

Precipitation is classified as snow or rain based on mp.rain_temperature_threshold.

Snowfall follows the layering of Crocus (Vionnet et al., 2012, p. 778; Lafaysse et al., 2026, eq. 11, p. 6278): it joins the top cell only when that cell is similar snow, and otherwise forms a layer of its own. Snow of mass P and depth z = P/ρ_new becomes a new top cell with fresh-snow properties when z > mp.column_dzmin, or when P is at least snow_layer_min_mass(cfs.dt) and it is distinct from the top cell (snow_layers_distinct at the snow_layer_distance D(n, 1) of the top cell and the fresh snow). Over firn or ice the distance is infinite, so such snow always forms its own cell. The new cell may be thinner than mp.column_dzmin; manage_layer_thickness keeps it while it stays distinct from the cell below and holds at least that mass.

Otherwise the snow merges into the top cell, of matrix mass M = dz[1]·density[1], whether that is snow, firn or ice: thickness grows by P/ρ_new and density becomes (M + P)/dz[1] (mass conserved); temperature is the enthalpy-weighted mix of the cell and the snow at the air temperature (mix_temperature; energy conserved); age is diluted by the age-0 snow (dilute_age); and the grain state is mixed by mass (mix_grains, weight f = P/(M + P) on the fresh snow). Snowfall below the minimum mass onto firn or ice therefore lowers the cell's density, so repeated light snowfall builds up snow there.

P = 0 changes nothing. A trace snowfall merges, being below the minimum mass. It moves the grain state by O(f), and not at all where the mixed values round back to the cell's own (5e-324 kg m-2 on any cell), and the density by O(f) (at most an ulp for 5e-324 kg m-2). The surface albedo, a function of the top cell's grain radius and density and of the snow mass above firn or ice, therefore moves by O(f) at most.

Rain is added to the pore water of the top cell, water[1], at the melt-point water enthalpy, and its sensible heat above the melting point, P·(h_w(T_air) − h_w(0 °C)), is added to the cell's ice matrix. The cell's density and thickness are unchanged. In a cold cell the rain refreezes in calculate_melt, where it is counted as refreeze; in a cell at the melting point its sensible heat melts ice there.

Returns (temperature, dz, density, water, grain_radius, grain_dendricity, grain_sphericity, age, rain). Arrays grow by one cell (via open_slot!) when the snow forms its own cell; the extra cell is reclaimed later in the timestep by manage_layer_thickness's count controller.

This is the model's principal age source. Every path adds mass at age 0: a fresh cell is set to 0 outright, and the merge-into-cell-1 paths dilute age[1] (the mean age of the cell's matrix and pore water together) by the arriving mass via dilute_age.

Albedo is absent: it is diagnosed from the column at the top of the next timestep (see calculate_albedo), which reads the fresh-snow grain size and density this function sets, so new snow brightens the surface without an albedo being stored here.

GEMB.vankampenhout_density_temperature Method
julia
vankampenhout_density_temperature(T_air) -> ρ_T [kg m-3]

Temperature term of the van Kampenhout et al. (2017) fresh-snow density, eq. 3, p. 2587, with T_air the near-surface air temperature [K] and ΔT = T_air - 273.15:

julia
ρ_T = 50 + 1.7·17^1.5                  ΔT > 2
      50 + 1.7·(ΔT + 15)^1.5           -15 < ΔT ≤ 2
      -3.8328·ΔT - 0.0333·ΔT²          ΔT ≤ -15

GEMB._albedo_gardner Method
julia
_albedo_gardner(grain_radius_surface, c, SZA, τ) -> α
_albedo_gardner(grain_radius_surface, c, SZA, τ, albedo_under, z_we) -> α

Broadband albedo from Gardner and Sharp (2010, eqs. 7–12, pp. 10–11): the pure-snow albedo α_S from the specific surface area (eq. 7), plus the changes due to soot loading c [ppmw] (eq. 8, giving α_c = α_S + dα_c), solar zenith angle SZA [degrees, 0 ≤ SZA < 90] (eqs. 9–10) and cloud optical thickness τ ≥ 0 (eq. 11), summed by eq. 12:

julia
α = α_c + dα_u(α_c) + dα_τ(α_c),
dα_u(α_c) = 0.53·α_S·(1 − α_c)·(1 − u′)^1.2,   dα_τ(α_c) = 0.1·τ·α_c^1.3 / (1 + 1.5τ)^α_S,

with u′ the cosine of the effective zenith angle (eq. 10). A zenith angle outside [0, 90) throws: the zenith term is defined only for the sun above the horizon.

The four-argument form is semi-infinite snow. The six-argument form is a snow layer z_we [m w.e.] deep over firn or ice whose exposed albedo is albedo_under, which carries no zenith or cloud term (firn_ice_albedo). Eq. 13 (p. 11 ¶51–52) replaces dα_c in eqs. 8–12 by

julia
dα′_c = (α_c^btm − α_S^top) + A·(α_c^top − α_c^btm),
A = min(1, 2.1·z^p),   p = 1.35·(1 − α_S^top) − 0.1·c^top − 0.13,

so α′_c = α_c^btm + A·(α_c^top − α_c^btm), with α_c^btm = albedo_under and the snow's α_S, α_c and c as the top layer. The zenith and cloud increments are evaluated at α′_c, as in the paper, and multiplied by A:

julia
α = α′_c + A·(dα_u(α′_c) + dα_τ(α′_c)).

Departure from the paper: the factor A on the two increments. Eq. 12 adds them unscaled, so as z → 0 the paper's albedo tends to eqs. 9–12 evaluated with α_c = α_c^btm, which lies above an exposed albedo that has no zenith or cloud term (by up to 0.45 for fresh snow at low sun under clear sky). With the factor, the z = 0 value is albedo_under exactly at every geometry and the albedo is continuous when the last snow goes. Where A = 1, from z_we = 2.1^(−1/p) upward, the factor is 1 and the result is the semi-infinite value of eqs. 7–12 bit for bit; z_we = Inf is semi-infinite snow. Inside the fitted depth range (z ≥ 0.01 m w.e.) A < 1 only where p > ln 2.1 / ln 100 ≈ 0.161, which for clean snow means r_e above about 0.18 mm.

Writing F = dα_u + dα_τ, F(α) ≤ k_u + k_τ on [0, 1] with k_u = 0.53·α_S·(1 − u′)^1.2 < 1 and k_τ = 0.1·τ/(1 + 1.5τ)^α_S, so |α − albedo_under| ≤ A·(|α_c^top − albedo_under| + k_u + k_τ): the albedo approaches the exposed value as A, i.e. as z^p. dα/dA = (α_c^top − albedo_under)·(1 + A·F′(α′_c)) + F(α′_c) with 1 + A·F′ ≥ 1 − k_u > 0, so where the snow's α_c is at least albedo_under the albedo increases with z. The approach is slow: p is 0.08–0.35 over the fitted grain sizes.

Gardner and Sharp fitted eq. 13 for snow over ice or old snow (p. 11 ¶52) with z 0.01–0.5 m w.e., r_e 0.05–1 mm, c 0–0.3 ppmw, SZA 0–75° and τ 0–20 (Table B2, p. 13). Below z = 0.01 m w.e. the formula is extrapolated toward A = 0. Over that fitted range p > 0; for p ≤ 0 (very fine or sooty snow, outside it) A is 1, which is the formula's value for every z_we ≤ 1 m w.e., so such snow keeps its semi-infinite albedo down to z = 0.

Calculate snow, firn, and ice albedo using one of several methods:

  1. "GardnerSharp": function of effective grain radius (Gardner & Sharp, 2010)

  2. "BrunLefebre": function of effective grain radius (Lefebre et al., 2003)

  3. "GreuellKonzelmann": function of density and cloud amount (Greuell & Konzelmann, 1994)

Returns (albedo, albedo_diffuse) as scalars — the broadband surface albedo for the direct-beam and for the diffuse stream.

Every method here is diagnostic: the albedo is a function of the current column state alone, so nothing is carried across a timestep boundary and nothing is stored per cell. albedo_diffuse differs from albedo only under :GardnerSharp (recomputed at a fixed 50° effective zenith angle); the other methods return it equal to albedo. calculate_shortwave_radiation applies albedo to the direct beam SW↓ − SW↓_diffuse and albedo_diffuse to SW↓_diffuse.

Under :GardnerSharp the direct-beam albedo uses cfs.solar_zenith_angle [degrees] and cfs.cloud_optical_thickness. When the sun is at or below the horizon (solar_zenith_angle ≥ 90°) there is no direct beam, and albedo is returned equal to albedo_diffuse. A step with SW↓ = 0 and the sun above the horizon is evaluated normally. Neither case changes the energy budget, since no shortwave is absorbed when SW↓ = 0.

Firn, ice, and snow over them

Under :GardnerSharp and :BrunLefebre the grain-size albedo describes semi-infinite snow. Where the column has a surface of firn or ice, or snow over one, the albedos are (firn_ice_albedo):

  • Exposed ice (density[1] ≥ mp.density_ice): mp.albedo_ice exactly, for both streams.

  • Exposed firn (DENSITY_PORE_CLOSEOFF ≤ density[1] < mp.density_ice): linear in density from mp.albedo_ice at mp.density_ice to ALBEDO_FIRN_MAX (0.65) at pore close-off, for both streams.

See _albedo_gardner.

  • Thin snow over firn or ice, :BrunLefebre: the MAR integrated albedo of Lefebre et al. (2003, p. 3 ¶14), linear in the snow thickness d between the albedo of the firn or ice surface at d = 0 and the semi-infinite snow albedo at d ≥ ALBEDO_SNOW_DEPTH_THICK (0.1 m).

GEMB.BARNOLA_CLOSEOFF Constant
julia
BARNOLA_CLOSEOFF

Density [kg m-3] at which _barnola_f switches from the fitted polynomial to the analytic closed-pore expression.

800, not GEMB's DENSITY_PORE_CLOSEOFF = 830: this is the density at which Barnola's polynomial fit is superseded by the closed-pore geometry, which is a property of that fit rather than of pore close-off as GEMB's other code means it. The polynomial is fitted to Pimienta and Duval's data over roughly 0.55-0.8 g cm-3 and diverges from the analytic form beyond it — at ρ = 900 it gives 0.0432 against the analytic 0.0087, a factor of 5 — which is why the handover exists rather than running the polynomial to ρᵢ.

The handover is meant to be smooth. The paper states that the polynomial "was calculated in order to make the two functions, fe(ρ) and fs(ρ), and their first derivatives equal for ρ = 0.8 g cm-3" — so both value and slope should match there, and any step is a mismatch rather than an intended feature of the law.

The size of that step depends on mp.density_ice, because only the closed-pore branch carries it. The two branches cross at ρᵢ = 919.96, and their first derivatives cross at 920.06 — the paper's C¹ statement is what fixes both, and it identifies the fit's own ice density as ~920 kg m-3. The branches agree to 0.06% at 920 against 4.3% at 917 and 14.1% at GEMB's default 910, so the gap at any other density_ice measures the mismatch between the configured value and the fit's. At the default 910 the rate steps down by 14% crossing 800, which is a discontinuity in dρ/dt but not in ρ, and is small against the factor-5 error the alternative would introduce deeper in the column.

The sign of that step is not fixed: it is downward below the fit's ~920 and upward above it (+7.6% at 925, +49.5% at 950). See BARNOLA_MIN_DENSITY_ICE for why the low side is gated and the high side is not.

BARNOLA_MIN_DENSITY_ICE

GEMB.BARNOLA_N Constant
julia
BARNOLA_N

Stress exponent in dρ/dt = ρ·A0·exp(-Q/RT)·f(ρ)·σⁿ.

Fixed at 3, matching Barnola et al. (1991) for the whole firn range this scheme covers: "n taken to be equal to 3, as the effective stress in the firn is rapidly higher than 0.1 MPa". Their A0 was fitted against n = 3 over 0.55-0.8 g cm-3, so the exponent and the prefactor are one calibration.

The paper's adjacent remark that "the exponent n is 1 when the effective stress is lower than 0.1 MPa" is not an unimplemented firn branch, though it reads like one. It scopes itself to bulk ice: "below the close-off, a good fit to the experimental data is obtained by taking successively n = 3 and n = 1 (Pimienta, 1987)", citing Doake and Wolff (1985) and Pimienta and Duval (1987). Those are shear-creep studies of dense polar ice — Pimienta and Duval torsion-tested 0.85 g cm-3 samples and analysed Dye 3 inclinometry at 1000-1784 m, and report no densification rate at all — so they supply no volumetric prefactor for porous firn, and none is published here to pair with n = 1.

Three reasons not to add the branch, beyond the missing prefactor:

  • Pimienta and Duval's own exponent is not 1. Their abstract and conclusion both give "smaller than 2"; regressing their Table 1 (South Pole ice, -15 °C) gives n ≈ 1.55. Barnola's "n is 1" rounds a sub-2 shear result.

  • Matching an n = 1 branch continuously at 0.1 MPa forces A1 = A0·(1e5)², so the rate would rise by (1e5/σ)² below it — ×2 at 70 kPa, ×41 at 15 kPa, ×100 at 10 kPa.

  • Barnola reproduced observed 0.55-0.83 g cm-3 profiles at Vostok and other Antarctic and Greenland sites with n = 3 throughout that range. A 40× shallow acceleration would break the agreement the coefficients exist to produce.

The Community Firn Model reaches the same conclusion, carrying the switch as dead code (# nBa[sigmaEff<1.0e5]=1.0).

Densification factor of Barnola et al. (1991), the geometric term in their eq. 2,

julia
dρ/dt = ρ · A0 · exp(-Q/RT) · f(ρ) · ΔPⁿ

Two regimes, meeting at BARNOLA_CLOSEOFF:

  • Open pores, ρ <= 800: the paper's eq. 3, fe(ρ), an empirical fit log10 f = α(ρ/1000)³ + β(ρ/1000)² + δ(ρ/1000) + γ "empirically deduced for the 0.55-0.8 g cm-3 density range", parameterizing the Pimienta and Duval (1987) pressure-sintering data.   • Closed pores, ρ > 800: the paper's eq. 4, fs(ρ), the Wilkinson and Ashby (1975) spherical pore model, f = (3/16)·(1 − ρ/ρᵢ) / (1 − (1 − ρ/ρᵢ)^(1/3))³, which the paper describes as "already valid below ρ = 0.8 g cm-3".

The identity was verified against the Community Firn Model's Barnola1991 zone 1 to a ratio of 1.0 (12 significant figures) across three (T, ρ, accumulation) triples, so :Barnola1991 and :HerronLangway share the one kernel rather than restating it.

CFM applies the full overburden σ where this uses the cell-midpoint value, matching the convention :Crocus already uses here; and CFM's zone 1 carries an A^aHL accumulation term with aHL = 1, which is the linear accumulation dependence already inside _hl_c0.

  • f1 = 1/(1 + 60·W_liq/(ρ_w·D)) (eq. 8) reduces viscosity in the presence of liquid water, bottoming out around 1/61 for a saturated cell. This is the term GEMB has no other expression of: every accumulation-driven scheme here is blind to water, and :ArthernB reads it only as overburden.

Eq. 9 is bounded above (min(4.0, ...)) but not below: it crosses 1 at gs = g2 = 0.2 mm and decays as exp((gs − g2)/g3) beneath that, so a fine-grained cell would get f2 < 1, a softening from a factor introduced for "the increase of viscosity with angular grains". Deviates from the Community Firn Model's Crocus, which was used to cross-check eq. 7 and the dρ/dt = ρσ/η form: CFM hardcodes f2 = 4.0 (eq. 9's ceiling, so it stiffens every cell including fresh fine-grained snow) and adopts van Kampenhout et al. Compute the densification of snow/firn using one of several models:

  • :HerronLangway: Herron and Langway (1980)

  • :Arthern: semi-empirical model of Arthern et al. (2010)

  • :ArthernB: physical model from Appendix B of Arthern et al. (2010)

  • :Crocus: viscous settling of Vionnet et al. (2012), handing firn to an accumulation-driven law (:Arthern by default) as mp.crocus_handoff_* set; the only scheme here in which liquid water weakens the matrix [default]

  • :CrocusPure: Vionnet et al. (2012) at every density, without the firn handover

  • :GSFC2020: recalibrated Arthern of Medley et al. (2022), GSFC-FDM v1.2.1

  • :Simonsen2013: Arthern retuned for Greenland by Simonsen et al. (2013)

  • :Barnola1991: Herron-Langway stage 1 below DENSITY_STAGE_TRANSITION, pressure sintering above; the only scheme here that models the firn-ice transition mechanistically

  • :LiZwally: empirical model of Li and Zwally (2004)

  • :Helsen: modified empirical model by Helsen et al. (2008)

  • :Ligtenberg: semi-empirical model of Ligtenberg et al. (2011)

Returns (dz, density). density is updated in place; dz is returned as a new array (recomputed from the conserved cell mass).

Pass a length-m viscosity vector to have the per-cell effective viscosity [Pa s] written into it. It is filled with NaN first and written only by the :Crocus/:CrocusPure settling branch, the one place a viscosity is genuinely formed; under every other scheme, and for the cells :Crocus hands to its firn law, it stays NaN. Omitting the keyword leaves the hot path untouched: the default is the empty NO_VISCOSITY, and an empty vector means "not requested" (a concrete type on both settings — see the note in gemb_core.jl).

With mp.destructive_metamorphism set, the :Crocus/:CrocusPure viscous branch adds the destructive-metamorphism settling of fresh snow (destructive_metamorphism_rate) to its overburden strain rate. Both are strain rates (1/D)·dD/dt, and van Kampenhout et al. (2017, Sect. 3.4) and Schneider et al. (2022, p. 3) sum them, so one step is D·exp(-(σ/η + r_dm)·dt) with both rates taken at the start-of-step state. The cells :Crocus hands to its firn law do not get the term; at the default handoff density of 450 it is below exp(-0.046·350) = 1e-7 of its fresh-snow value. Requesting it with any other densification scheme throws.

water [kg m-2] is read only by the stress-driven schemes: :ArthernB and :Barnola1991 (as part of the overburden) and :Crocus/:CrocusPure (overburden, eq. 8's viscosity reduction, and the grain-type gate of eq. 9); the accumulation-driven schemes proxy overburden with mean accumulation and ignore it.

grain_dendricity and grain_sphericity (keywords, per cell) are read only by :Crocus/:CrocusPure, which apply eq. 9's grain-type stiffening only to cells that crocus_f2_applies admits. Those two schemes throw an ArgumentError when either is missing or of the wrong length; every other scheme ignores them.

The accumulation-driven branches are scalar-loop implementations, numerically identical element by element to the vectorized form they replaced but without its mask / gather / broadcast temporaries (mass_init, idx, H, c0, c1, ...) allocated per call.

GEMB.crocus_f2_applies Method

crocus_f2_applies(dendricity, sphericity, water, grain_radius) -> Bool

Whether eq. 9's grain-type factor f2 of the Crocus viscosity applies to a cell. It applies when the cell is

  • non-dendritic: dendricity at most GDN_TOLERANCE (the classification calculate_grain_size uses) and an optical diameter d of at least 1e-4·(4 − S) m, Crocus's own non-dendritic test, with d from optical_diameter (Lafaysse et al., 2026, eq. D5, GEMB's grain size 2·grain_radius);

  • angular: sphericity < CROCUS_F2_SPHERICITY_MAX = 51/99;

  • dry: water at most WATER_TOLERANCE [kg m-2].

Every other cell gets f2 = 1. The optical-diameter test admits only grains of at least 0.33 mm (0.40 mm at sphericity 0), where eq. 9 is 3.7 to 4: angular snow finer than that, which GEMB's :FlannerZender2006 radius readily produces, is dendritic snow to Crocus and gets no stiffening there either.

Crocus 3.0.1 (snowcro.F90:2112-2121, SNOWCROCOMPACTN) applies f2 only to a layer that is non-dendritic by that optical-diameter test, has sphericity below 51/99, and carries the historical tracker value 1: faceted crystals at some time and liquid water never (Lafaysse et al., 2026, Table 2). The tracker is set to 1 when a dry non-dendritic layer first reaches sphericity below 0.1/99 (snowcro.F90:2596-2598), and wetting that rounds the grains moves it to 3, after which f2 never applies again (snowcro.F90:2600-2603). Neither Vionnet et al. (2012, eq. 9) nor Lafaysse et al. (2026, eq. 55) prints the history condition; the code is the reference implementation.

GEMB carries no historical tracker, so this gate reads the cell's present state. It differs from Crocus in three ways:

  • coarse angular snow that never reached sphericity 0 is stiffened here and not in Crocus;

  • a layer that was wet and has refrozen and faceted again is stiffened here and not in Crocus, which excludes it for good;

  • a faceted layer that holds water but has not yet rounded to sphericity 1 is excluded here and stiffened in Crocus, where f1 then dominates its viscosity anyway.

The first two err toward stiffening, the third toward softening.

GEMB.destructive_metamorphism_rate Method
julia
destructive_metamorphism_rate(density, temperature, wet, density_dm) -> r [s-1]

Compaction rate -(1/D)·dD/dt of a snow layer by destructive metamorphism, the settling of fresh snow as its crystals lose their branches. Anderson (1976) in the form of van Kampenhout et al. (2017) eq. 5, p. 2587, with the density taper of Schneider et al. (2022) eq. 3, p. 3:

julia
r = c3·c2·c1·exp(-c4·(T_frz - min(T, T_frz)))
c1 = 1                           ρ < ρ_dm
     exp(-c5·(ρ - ρ_dm))          ρ ≥ ρ_dm
c2 = 2 with liquid water, else 1

with c3 = 2.777e-6 s-1, c4 = 0.04 K-1 (van Kampenhout et al., p. 2587) and c5 = 0.046 m3 kg-1 (Schneider et al., p. 3). density is the ice-matrix density of the layer, excluding liquid water, as eq. 5 specifies; ρ_dm is mp.destructive_metamorphism_density, 175 kg m-3 in CLM5. wet selects c2 = 2, which the papers state as "depending on the presence of liquid water" without a threshold; any pore water above WATER_TOLERANCE counts.

The paper prints eq. 5 as ∂ρ/∂t but its units are s-1: it is the fractional rate that Schneider et al. eq. 3 writes as a strain rate, and the one the Community Land Model sums with the overburden strain rate. Temperature is capped at T_frz, so wet snow at the melting point takes the largest rate, 2·c3. Below ρ_dm the rate is c3·exp(-c4·ΔT) and does not depend on density; above it the rate falls by a factor e every 21.7 kg m-3, so the term moves fresh snow to ρ_dm within days and then stops mattering.

Throws an ArgumentError for a non-positive density or a non-finite or non-positive temperature.

GEMB.calculate_grain_size Method
julia
calculate_grain_size(temperature, dz, density, water, grain_radius, grain_dendricity, grain_sphericity, cfs::ClimateForcingStep, mp::ModelParameters)

Model the evolution of effective snow grain size, dendricity, and sphericity.

Accounts for different physical processes depending on snow state:

  • Dendritic Snow (fresh): Evolves based on temperature gradients and liquid water content.

  • Nondendritic Dry Snow: Temperature gradient metamorphism using Marbouty (1980), or normal grain growth after Arthern et al. (2010), selected by mp.grain_growth_method.

  • Wet Snow: Rapid grain growth due to liquid water using Brun (1989).

Except under :FlannerZender2006, mp.grain_growth_method governs the non-dendritic dry branch only; the dendritic and wet branches, and the sphericity caps, are the same on every other setting.

  • :Marbouty — Marbouty (1980) everywhere. Its density factor H vanishes at DENSITY_MARBOUTY_MAX, so grain radius is frozen throughout the firn column below a few metres.

  • :Arthern — dr²/dt = kgr·exp(-Eg/RT) (GRAIN_GROWTH_KGR, GRAIN_GROWTH_EG) at every density, dropping the temperature-gradient dependence entirely. Fitted to firn, it barely coarsens dry surface snow.

  • :hybrid — Marbouty below DENSITY_MARBOUTY_MAX, Arthern at or above it. Seasonal snow keeps the temperature-gradient physics; firn grains keep growing, which matters because :ArthernB densification goes as 1/r².

  • :Taillandier2007 — below DENSITY_MARBOUTY_MAX, the SSA decay of dry snow of Taillandier et al. (2007), equitemperature or temperature-gradient by the cell's gradient (taillandier_ssa); Arthern at or above it.

  • :FlannerZender2006 (the default) — the snow aging of SNICAR in CLM5.0 (snicar_aging_radius): the radius of every dendritic cell and every cell below DENSITY_MARBOUTY_MAX, dry or wet, grows by the Flanner and Zender (2006) law with the parameters of the SNICAR table at the cell's temperature, gradient and density, plus CLM's wet growth, and is capped at SNICAR_RADIUS_MAX in place of the sphericity caps. Radius is then state of its own for dendritic snow too, so it is no longer diagnosed from dendricity and sphericity, here or where cells mix (independent_grain_radius); dendricity and sphericity still evolve as on every other setting, for the schemes that read them. Refrozen water enters at SNICAR_RADIUS_REFROZEN (snicar_refreeze!, from calculate_melt). Cells at or above DENSITY_MARBOUTY_MAX follow Arthern as under :hybrid.

Under :Marbouty and :hybrid dry non-dendritic snow below 400 kg m-3 grows only where the gradient exceeds 16 K m-1 (Marbouty's G is 0 below it), and at the 350 kg m-3 of GEMB's fresh snow by at most 0.2 of the 0.09 mm d-1 of Marbouty's rate (his H). This follows Brun et al. (1992) as used in Crocus (Vionnet et al., 2012, Table 1), where non-dendritic snow does not grow at gradients up to 15 K m-1: equitemperature coarsening of non-dendritic snow is represented only by :Taillandier2007 and :FlannerZender2006.

Runs unconditionally. Metamorphism is not contingent on model configuration — grains coarsen in a real snowpack whichever albedo scheme is selected — and grain_radius is read by four schemes across three modules (calculate_albedo, calculate_shortwave_radiation, :ArthernB/:Crocus/:CrocusPure densification in calculate_density, and the grain-radius emissivity methods in calculate_temperature). Gating the call on albedo_method (as this once did, and as the MATLAB model still does) covers only the first two, leaving the other configurations to run with grain size frozen at the initial profile — silently. Enumerating the consumers to skip the work costs 11% on the runs that can skip it and re-breaks silently whenever a new consumer is added, so the work is simply always done.

Returns (grain_radius, grain_dendricity, grain_sphericity). All three are updated in place, and the returned grain_radius is the same array that was passed in — pass a copy if the incoming values are still needed. grain_radius used to be returned as a fresh array; it now shares the mutation convention of the other two, which removed the largest allocation in the function (see the gsz comment below).

This is a scalar-loop implementation, numerically identical element by element to the vectorized form it replaced but without the ~30 mask / gather / broadcast temporaries that form allocated per call.

Specific surface area of dry snow after dt_hours of metamorphism at temperature T [K] under a temperature gradient gradient [K m-1], from Taillandier et al. (2007, p. 8):

julia
SSA(t) = B − A·ln(t + Δt),   Δt = exp((B − SSA0)/A),   t in hours,

with, for mean temperature Tm [°C] and initial SSA SSA0, equitemperature metamorphism (eqs. 10-13, gradient ≤ SSA_DECAY_GRADIENT_THRESHOLD)

julia
A = 0.0760·SSA0 − 1.76·(Tm − 2.96),   B = 0.629·SSA0 − 15.0·(Tm − 11.2),

and temperature-gradient metamorphism (eqs. 6-9, above it)

julia
A = 0.0961·SSA0 − 3.44·(Tm + 1.90),   B = 0.659·SSA0 − 27.2·(Tm − 2.03).

SSA0 is GEMB's fresh-snow SSA, SSA_NEW_SNOW, and Tm the temperature at this step. GEMB._refreeze_cell Method
julia
_refreeze_cell(mp, melt_input, water, freeze_max, M, density, dz) ->
    (freeze1, freeze2, water_delta, flux_out, M_new, density_new)

Refreeze, retention and through-flow of melt_input [kg m-2] of water entering a cold cell of ice mass M, density density, thickness dz (held fixed) and pore water water. freeze_max is the cell's cold content as a mass of water.

  • freeze1 = min(melt_input, (ρᵢ − ρ)·dz, freeze_max) of the incoming water refreezes.

  • The pore water then moves toward the irreducible content at the new density, by water_delta = clamp(water_irr − water, −water, melt_input − freeze1).

  • When the cell expels water (water_delta < 0), up to the remaining cold content and pore space of the expelled water also refreezes, freeze2.

  • What is left continues downward: flux_out = melt_input − freeze1 − freeze2 − water_delta, so melt_input = freeze1 + freeze2 + water_delta + flux_out exactly.

GEMB.aquifer_diagnostics Method
julia
aquifer_diagnostics(dz, density, water, mp) -> (thickness, depth)

Summarize the standing (super-irreducible) water in a column.

thickness [m] is the total thickness of cells holding more water than capillary forces can retain — the saturated thickness a borehole or a firn core would find wet. depth [m] is the depth to the top of the shallowest such cell, NaN when there are none, so "dry column" stays distinguishable from "water table at the surface".

Both are zero and NaN under mp.runoff_method === :instantaneous, where no cell can hold more than its irreducible water. They are what makes the ponding in calculate_melt observable in output, and the quantity RetMIP (Vandecrux et al., 2020, Sect. 5.4) evaluates aquifer representation against.

A cell counts as saturated only once it exceeds irreducible by AQUIFER_TOLERANCE of its pore space, rather than by the arithmetic WATER_TOLERANCE the physics uses. calculate_melt leaves a retaining cell at exactly irreducible, but calculate_density then compacts it within the same timestep, shrinking the pore space that retention was computed against and leaving the cell genuinely — if negligibly — over-saturated. That residue accumulates between melt events and would otherwise read as a metres-thick water table in a run that cannot have one. It is separable from real ponding by saturation but not by mass: the residue sits ~1e-7 of pore space above irreducible against 1e-1 or more for ponded water.

Diagnostic only: nothing in the physics reads either value. Called from gemb_core after the grid controllers, alongside ice_slab_diagnostics, so the column scanned is the one the profile output records at that timestamp.

GEMB.calculate_melt Method
julia
calculate_melt(temperature, dz, density, water, grain_radius, grain_dendricity, grain_sphericity, age, mp::ModelParameters, verbose::Bool)

Compute meltwater generation, percolation, refreezing, and runoff using a tipping bucket approach.

Processes:

  1. Initial Refreeze: Existing pore water in cold layers is refrozen.

  2. Melt Generation: Excess energy above 0 C is converted to liquid meltwater.

  3. Percolation: Liquid water percolates downward, refreezing in cold layers, being retained as pore water, or running off at impermeable ice lenses.

Water is routed to runoff at a contiguous run of cells at or above mp.impermeable_density thicker than mp.impermeable_thickness (defaults 830 kg m-3 and 0.1 m; across the RetMIP models the density criterion spans 810–917 kg m-3 — see ModelParameters).

Ponding above a barrier

What happens to water that reaches a barrier depends on mp.runoff_method:

  • :instantaneous (the default) — it leaves the column in the same timestep. Every cell is capped at its irreducible saturation, so the column can never hold standing water.

  • :ZuoOerlemans / :Darcy — it backs up. After the downward percolation loop, an upward pass fills the cells above each barrier to their pore capacity mp.pore_saturation_max·(ρᵢ − ρ)·dz [kg m-2], deepest first; only what is left after cell 1 is full genuinely leaves as runoff, the column then being saturated to the surface. Water in excess of irreducible instead drains laterally, over a finite timescale, in apply_lateral_drainage!. Barrier cells are excluded from the fill: at or above mp.impermeable_density there is no connected pore space to pond in, which is the premise of their being impermeable in the first place. The capacity uses the same (ρᵢ − ρ)·S·dz form as irreducible_saturation with S = mp.pore_saturation_max, so water/capacity is exactly the saturation S_wi is defined against and a cell at S_wi = 1 reads as full rather than over-full. The upward pass does not refreeze what it deposits, even into a cell that is still cold (a cell can pass water down while retaining cold content if its refreeze was capped by d_max rather than by cold content). Any such cell is resolved at the top of the next timestep by the REFREEZE PORE WATER block above, so the only cost is a one-step delay; mass and energy are conserved either way, because pore water and runoff carry the same melt-point enthalpy. Firn aquifers then form bottom-up with no further machinery: water still drains to irreducible everywhere it passes, piles up at the deepest barrier, and backs up from there. RetMIP (Vandecrux et al., 2020, Sect. 5.4) excluded the firn-aquifer site from its retention evaluation for exactly this reason — models that cannot hold water above irreducible saturation cannot represent an aquifer at all.

No preferential-flow domain

This is a single-domain bucket scheme: water descends cell by cell through the matrix, and there is no second, fast, heterogeneous ("piping") flow path, nor a Richards-equation matrix solver. That is deliberate. RetMIP (Vandecrux et al., 2020) found that the three of nine models carrying an explicit deep- or preferential-percolation scheme (CFM-Cr, CFM-KM, UppsalaUniDeepPerc) did worse than the bucket schemes at three of the four Greenland sites: they infiltrated too deeply and ran warm — firn-temperature mean error +3.6 to +6.2 °C at Dye-2 and +1.8 to +4.7 °C at KAN_U, plus a warm bias at the near-melt-free Summit site — and at Dye-2 in 2016 they percolated to 10 m against the 2.5 m the upward-looking radar observed, growing near-surface ice slabs several metres thick where none exist. Their one advantage was the firn-aquifer site, which only they recharged. RetMIP's conclusion (their Sect. 5.2) is that until preferential flow in firn is better constrained observationally, the more complex schemes do not necessarily give better results than simple bucket schemes; the productive levers for a bucket scheme are instead the impermeability criterion and the runoff timescale above — the two best-performing models at the ice-slab site KAN_U were bucket schemes that delay runoff rather than models that percolate deeper.

Returns (temperature, dz, density, water, grain_radius, grain_dendricity, grain_sphericity, age, melt_total, melt_surface, runoff_total, freeze_total, percolation_depth).

melt_total is the ice melted this timestep and freeze_total all liquid water refrozen, including rain, which arrives as pore water (see calculate_accumulation). Over any period melt + rain − refreeze − runoff equals the change in column liquid water.

Age transport

age [d] is the mass-weighted mean age of all mass in a cell, matrix and pore water together, so a phase change that stays inside one cell (the initial pore-water refreeze) does not move it at all. Melt removal is likewise age-neutral: it takes a fraction of the cell, which leaves the mean age of the remainder unchanged.

What does move age is percolation. Meltwater carries the age of the firn it came from, so the percolation loop transports the age moment mass × age alongside flux_dn, in a companion flux_age vector holding the mass-weighted mean age of the water in transit. At each cell the water generated there and any water the cell expels (both at age[i]) mix with the through-flow from above (at flux_age[i]) into one pool; whatever refreezes enters the cell at the pool's age, and whatever continues down or runs off leaves at it. Refreezing therefore makes a cell younger only to the extent that genuinely younger water reached it, rather than resetting it.

percolation_depth [m] is a diagnostic that takes no part in the mass or energy budget: the base of the deepest cell water reached this timestep, 0 when no water moved. Note that the percolation loop can walk past cells water never entered (its exit also waits on the deepest cell holding pre-existing excess pore water), so this tracks water arrival per cell rather than the loop index. The companion slab diagnostics are taken in gemb_core via ice_slab_diagnostics, after the grid controllers run.

Arrays may shrink (cells deleted when mass=0).

basal_surplus, when given, is a column resting on ground (lower_boundary = :ground): energy beyond what melts the deepest cell entirely is added to basal_surplus[] [J m-2] for the ground to take, and the deepest cell melts out. Without it that case is an error.

GEMB.irreducible_saturation Method
julia
irreducible_saturation(mp::ModelParameters, density) -> S_wi [-]

Irreducible (capillary-held) water saturation of the pore space, per mp.water_irreducible_method. The retention of a cell is (ρᵢ − ρ) · S_wi · dz [kg m-2] at every site that uses it, so only S_wi varies between methods.

  • :constant — mp.water_irreducible_saturation at every density (Colbeck, 1974).

  • :ColeouLesaffre — the default. Coléou and Lesaffre (1998) eq. 3 via Langen et al. (2017) eq. 4, where irreducible water mass per unit total mass is wmi = 0.057(ρᵢ − ρ)/ρ + 0.017 and

    S_wi = wmi/(1 − wmi) · ρᵢ·ρ / (ρ_w(ρᵢ − ρ))

    mp.water_irreducible_saturation is ignored. Retention rises with density — ~0.069 at ρ = 300 against ~0.163 at ρ = 800 (ρᵢ = 917) — which is where the constant value under-retains most, in the percolation zone.

The gate also removes the ρ → ρᵢ singularity in S_wi, which is why it takes the min: with mp.density_ice configured below pore closeoff the singularity would otherwise sit below the constant threshold.

The gate is the constant DENSITY_PORE_CLOSEOFF, deliberately not the tunable mp.impermeable_density. The two thresholds answer different questions: this one is where capillary retention ceases for want of connected pore space, while impermeable_density is where a lens stops conducting flow at the scale the model represents. Tying them together would make lowering the flow criterion silently change retention too.

GEMB._max_safe_dt Method
julia
_max_safe_dt(temperature, dz, density, K, mp)

Largest explicit sub-step [s] that keeps the thermal solve stable. Allocation-free.

The scheme in calculate_temperature updates cell i as H[i] += F_i - F_{i-1} with F_i = G_i·dt·(T_{i+1} - T_i), so the coefficient of T_i in the equivalent temperature update is 1 - dt·(G_i + G_{i-1})/(ρᵢcᵢdzᵢ). Requiring it to stay non-negative — the positivity condition that is Von Neumann stability for this stencil — gives

julia
dt ≤ ρᵢ cᵢ dzᵢ / (Gᵢ + Gᵢ₋₁),    Gᵢ = 1 / (dz[i+1]/(2K[i+1]) + dz[i]/(2K[i]))

evaluated here by reusing the very harmonic-mean face conductances the solve uses, so the limit and the scheme cannot drift apart.

This replaced the textbook uniform-grid form 0.5·ρᵢcᵢdzᵢ²/Kᵢ, which substitutes 2Kᵢ/dzᵢ for Gᵢ + Gᵢ₋₁. The two agree exactly when dz and K are uniform, but GEMB's grid is graded, and the substitution is not conservative there: since Gᵢ ≤ 2Kᵢ/dzᵢ with equality only in the limit of a vanishing neighbour resistance, Gᵢ + Gᵢ₋₁ can reach 4Kᵢ/dzᵢ, so the old form could overestimate the true limit by up to a factor of two. Measured per cell on GEMB's own column the ratio spanned 0.66 to 1.82 — an error in both directions, the low end of which the 0.8 safety factor does not cover. It is also looser at the cell that binds, so the correct limit is both sound and cheaper.

Cell m is excluded: it is the Dirichlet reservoir, its enthalpy is never updated (Q_sw[m] = 0, no flux is drawn from it), so it imposes no stability constraint. The old form included it.

Diffusion only: the surface energy balance on cell 1 is linearized in the cell's new temperature, which keeps its feedback stable at any sub-step (see _thermal_solve!); _surface_relaxation_dt bounds the step for the accuracy of that flux.

GEMB._thermal_solve! Method
julia
_thermal_solve!(::ImplicitThermal, temperature, dz, density, K, shortwave_flux, water_surface, grain_radius, sfc, cfs, mp, verbose)

Advance the column temperature over one forcing timestep with a backward-Euler scheme on a tridiagonal system, solved by the Thomas algorithm. Unconditionally stable, so it takes no stability sub-steps: _max_safe_dt, _find_dt_divisor, mp.dt_divisors and DT_MIN_WARN are all unused on this path, and a thin refrozen lens costs nothing.

temperature is updated in place; the return is the AbstractThermalSolver 6-tuple. water_surface and grain_radius are carried for the verbose diagnostic only.

The system

Unknowns are cells 1 … m-1. Cell m is the Dirichlet reservoir and is never an unknown, so its temperature is returned bit-unchanged by construction; it enters row m-1 as the known term A_face[m-1]·temperature[m] on the right-hand side. Its shortwave share is absorbed by cell m-1, exactly as on the explicit path.

Row i is the backward-Euler enthalpy balance M_i·(h(T_i) − h(T_i^old)) = Q_sw,i + F_i − F_{i-1} with the diffusive fluxes evaluated at the new temperatures, giving diagonal M_i·c_i + A_face[i-1] + A_face[i] and off-diagonals −A_face[i]. The face conductances A_face are the same harmonic means the explicit path uses, so the two schemes discretize identical conductances and differ only in time integration.

c_i is the chord_heat_capacity between the old and new temperatures — an exact identity for h = aT + (b/2)T², not a linearization. This is what lets enthalpy stay the conserved quantity while the system solved is linear in temperature. It also makes the system mildly nonlinear under the default :CuffeyPaterson, resolved by the same iteration as the surface row; under :constant it is a true constant and the iteration only sees the surface.

M_i·c_i > 0 always, so the matrix is an irreducibly diagonally dominant M-matrix: non-singular, no pivoting required, and A⁻¹ ≥ 0, which is the discrete maximum principle — the solve cannot manufacture a new extremum.

The surface row: Newton, not lagged Picard

The surface flux Q(T_1) is strongly nonlinear and must be taken implicitly. Lagging it — Picard iteration on the previous iterate's flux — diverges here: the fixed-point gain |dQ/dT_1|·Δt/(M_1 c_1) exceeds 1 for a centimetre-scale surface cell at any Δt of interest. (The explicit path's lagged sub-steps are stable for the converse reason: their tiny dt drives the same gain far below 1.)

So the flux is linearized into the diagonal, Q(T_1) ≈ Q_k + Λ_k(T_1 − T_{1,k}) with Λ_k ≤ 0 from _surface_energy_balance_and_slope, which strengthens the already dominant diagonal. Since Q_k is the true nonlinear flux at the iterate, the two Λ terms cancel at convergence and the residual satisfied is the true nonlinear surface balance. Λ therefore affects only the convergence rate, never the answer — the licence for its clamp.

Both discontinuities are held as far outside the iteration as possible: the emissivity melt switch is evaluated once per sub-step, as on the explicit path, and the LV/LS latent-heat switch is absorbed by the Λ clamp. That is not quite sufficient — the latent-heat switch still enters through Q_k itself — so a fraction of solves end in small limit cycles across 273.15 K rather than converging, and the iteration is damped: the step weight halves whenever a step fails to shrink, contracting a cycle onto its own mean, and the least-residual iterate is retained so a cycle still running at the cap is not left on its worse phase. Converging solves are bit-identical under this, the weight never leaving 1 on the quadratic path. See THERMAL_IMPLICIT_DAMPING_FLOOR for the measurement.

Conservation

A Thomas sweep leaves a round-off residual, so the solved field is used as a predictor of the implicit face temperatures rather than written to temperature directly. The enthalpy update is applied as one flux per face, +F to cell i and −F to cell i+1 — structurally the same pass the explicit path uses. Pairwise cancellation is exact to the last bit, so the column total conserves independently of the solve residual, the Newton tolerance, and c_p(T), and the verbose budget check is the explicit path's with no tolerance moved.

The flux applied at the surface in that pass is the true nonlinear flux at the converged iterate, not its linearization, and the returned averages are those same applied values — so gemb_core's energy budget and the evaporation_condensation mass budget close by construction.

Wet cells

A cell holding water is on the freezing plateau of _wet_cell_temperature while its water is partly frozen, so its temperature is not a function of its enthalpy there. Each wet cell's row is therefore written for one of three states (_wet_cell_mode): all water liquid (the matrix's heat capacity, temperature free to rise above the melting point), on the plateau (the row is T_i = CtoK and the energy it receives goes to freezing or melting its water), or all water frozen (the heat capacity of matrix and water together). After each solve the enthalpy every wet cell would receive is classified again; where the state differs, the row is rewritten and the sub-step solved again, at most twice per wet cell, and a state that has not settled by then throws. A dry column takes none of these passes and is unchanged by them.

Sub-stepping

Sub-steps here buy accuracy, not stability: backward Euler is first-order and strongly damping, and the emissivity melt switch only resolves between sub-steps. The count follows THERMAL_IMPLICIT_DT_TARGET and is independent of the cell count, of dz, and of the stiffest cell — the property the explicit path lacks.

Static condensation: Newton on a scalar, not on the column

The nonlinearity is confined to row 1 — the surface energy balance. Rows 2 … n are linear in the iterate for a given set of chord heat capacities, so re-eliminating them once per Newton iteration would be wasted work. Instead the interior is condensed bottom-up, once per sub-step, each row reduced to an affine function of the cell above it (T_i = rhs[i] + sup[i]·T_{i-1}). Newton then iterates on a single scalar equation in T_1, at O(1) per iteration, and one forward substitution propagates the answer back down the column. The operator is constant across all Newton iterations of a sub-step, so it is factorized once and reused.

Under :CuffeyPaterson the chord capacities depend on the iterate, so the condensation is repeated in an outer loop until they settle; under :constant the outer loop provably runs once.

Cost

Slower than the explicit path on a column with no stiff cell (~2.4x at the default sub-step target), and insensitive to the stiffest cell where the explicit path is not — which is the whole reason to select it. See the Thermal solvers page for the measured runtimes, the condensation speedup, and the optimizations that were tried and rejected.

GEMB.calculate_temperature Function
julia
calculate_temperature(temperature, dz, density, water_surface, grain_radius, shortwave_flux, cfs::ClimateForcingStep, mp::ModelParameters, verbose::Bool, workspace=ThermalWorkspace(); water=nothing)

Compute new temperature profile accounting for energy absorption and thermal diffusion.

Solves the 1D heat transfer equation with a finite-volume scheme (Patankar, 1980), explicit or implicit per mp.thermal_solver. Accounts for:

  • Surface energy balance (turbulent fluxes, radiative fluxes)

  • Subsurface thermal diffusion

  • Shortwave penetration as a source term

  • Thermal conductivity updates (per mp.thermal_conductivity_method)

Sub-time steps are determined by Von Neumann stability analysis.

Returns (temperature, longwave_upward, heat_flux_sensible, heat_flux_latent, heat_flux_basal, evaporation_condensation, evaporation_condensation_liquid, refrozen).

evaporation_condensation [kg m-2, positive for deposition or condensation] is the vapor mass exchanged over the step. evaporation_condensation_liquid is the part exchanged in sub-steps whose latent heat was LV (a surface at the melting point with liquid water to give, see _surface_liquid), which is exchanged with liquid water; the rest, at LS, is exchanged with ice. See apply_vapor_exchange!.

longwave_upward [W m-2, positive upward] is emitted plus reflected longwave, εσT_s⁴ + (1 − ε)·LW↓, with ε the emissivity in force at each sub-step. The surface therefore absorbs ε·LW↓, and LW↓ − longwave_upward is the net longwave the column received.

Liquid water as a latent buffer

water [kg m-2 per cell], when given, is the column's pore water, with water[1] == water_surface. A cell holding water cannot cool below the melting point inside the solve: its enthalpy counts the water as liquid, and cooling first freezes that water at 273.15 K (_wet_cell_temperature) before the cell's temperature falls. The surface fluxes and the diffusion see that temperature.

The returned temperature of a wet cell is normally its ice matrix temperature with all of its water still counted as liquid. A cell whose water has partly frozen inside the solve is returned below 273.15 K with exactly the cold content that freezing released. calculate_melt refreezes that water at the start of its pass and counts it as refreeze, which returns the cell to the state the solve ended in.

The exception is a cell whose matrix would have to go below WET_HANDOFF_TEMPERATURE_MIN to carry that cold content. There the solve freezes the water itself (_wet_cell_handoff!), changing water, density and dz in place. refrozen [kg m-2] is the mass frozen this way over the column; per cell it is in workspace.refrozen, and the caller counts it as refreeze. water = nothing treats the column as dry.

workspace supplies the solver's scratch buffers (see ThermalWorkspace). The default allocates a fresh one per call, which is convenient for a one-off call but allocates every time; gemb passes a single workspace down for the whole run.

GEMB.chord_heat_capacity Method
julia
chord_heat_capacity(mp::ModelParameters, T1, T2) -> c_chord [J kg-1 K-1]

Heat capacity that makes the enthalpy difference between two temperatures an exact product:

julia
specific_enthalpy(mp, T2) - specific_enthalpy(mp, T1) == c_chord * (T2 - T1)

For c_p = a + bT the enthalpy is h = aT + (b/2)T², so the secant slope of h between T1 and T2 is a + b·(T1 + T2)/2 — the heat capacity evaluated at the midpoint. This is an algebraic identity, not a linearization: h is quadratic, so its chord slope is exact for any T1, T2, however far apart. Under :constant it is mp.heat_capacity_ice, and it reduces to heat_capacity(mp, T) when T1 == T2.

This is what lets an implicit thermal solver carry enthalpy as the conserved quantity while solving a linear system in temperature. Writing M·c_p(T_old)·(T_new − T_old) instead — the obvious lagged form — injects a spurious (b/2)(T_new − T_old)² of energy per step, which is the error the specific_enthalpy warning describes in its per-cell form. Only the midpoint evaluation cancels it.

c_p > 0 over the physical range, so c_chord > 0 and the diagonal it scales stays positive.

See specific_enthalpy for the enthalpy this is the chord slope of.

GEMB._thermal_conductivity_calonne2019 Method
julia
_thermal_conductivity_calonne2019(T, ρ; air_factor::Bool) -> K [W m-1 K-1]

Calonne et al. (2019) eq. 5. A logistic weight θ in density blends two regime-specific fits, each rescaled by the constituent conductivities at T relative to their values at the temperature the fits were calibrated at (CONDUCTIVITY_T_REF = 270.15 K):

julia
θ      = 1/(1 + exp(-2a(ρ - ρ_t))),      a = 0.02, ρ_t = 450 kg m-3
k_snow = 0.024 - 1.23e-4ρ + 2.5e-6ρ^2   (the :Calonne 2011 fit, ρ -> 0 gives air)
k_firn = 2.107 + 0.003618(ρ - 917)       (ρ -> 917 gives ice)
K      = (1-θ)·(K_ice/k_i)·(K_air/k_a)·k_snow + θ·(K_ice/k_i)·k_firn

The two regimes carry different scalings because they are physically different: the firn fit describes a connected ice skeleton and so scales with ice alone, while the snow fit describes grains in air and scales with both constituents. The reference values are internally consistent with that reading — k_i = 2.107 is _thermal_conductivity_ice at 270.15 K to within 1.2e-4 relative, and k_a = 0.024 is _thermal_conductivity_air there to within 6.6e-3 — so the ratios are 1 at the reference temperature by construction and the fits return their published values.

air_factor selects which of the two published readings of the snow term is used:

  • true (:Calonne2019Air, the default) carries K_air(T)/K_air(T_ref), as eq. 5 is printed (p. 13,087). This matches IMAU-FDM (firn_physics.f90, Thermal_Cond). Since it enters only as a ratio, Reid's absolute calibration cancels and only its temperature shape matters — which is why the 0.7% gap between K_air(T_ref) and k_a is irrelevant here.

  • false (:Calonne2019) pins K_air at its reference value, so the snow prefactor collapses to K_ice/k_i and the whole blend takes a single factor. This matches the Community Firn Model, whose diffusion.py hardcodes K_air = kref_a with a standing TODO to find the temperature dependence.

Air conducts more poorly as it cools, so the ratio is below 1 below the reference temperature (0.83 at 220 K, 0.88 at 233 K, 0.94 at 253 K) and :Calonne2019 therefore returns a higher conductivity than :Calonne2019Air for cold snow — by up to ~20% at 220 K for ρ ≲ 300, ~10% at ρ = 450, and under 0.3% by ρ = 550 where θ → 1 and the air-free firn branch takes over. Against eq. 5 evaluated with the paper's own constituent conductivities at −20 and −60 °C (p. 13,081), :Calonne2019Air at ρ = 200 is within 3%, while :Calonne2019 is 27% high at −60 °C.

Both forms are continuous into ice: at ρ = 917 the weight is 1 to within 1e-8 and k_firn = k_i, so K = K_ice(T) to the same relative precision and no ice branch is needed. The air factor cannot disturb this because it is weighted by (1-θ).

Unlike most density constants in GEMB this uses the literal 917, not mp.density_ice: it is a fitted coefficient of the published regression, not a configurable property of the column (the same reasoning as the :Barnola1991 note in initialize_parameters.jl).

Cross-checked against the Community Firn Model's Calonne2019 conductivity in CFM_main/diffusion.py and IMAU-FDM's Thermal_Cond in source/firn_physics.f90.

GEMB.obukhov_stability Function
julia
obukhov_stability(Ri, z_w, z_T, z0, zT, logM=log(z_w/z0), logHT=log(z_T/zT)) -> ζ

Stability parameter ζ = z_w/L at the wind observation height z_w, from the bulk Richardson number Ri = g·Δθ·z_w/(θ̄·U²) (Δθ between the temperature height z_T and the surface, U at z_w).

Monin-Obukhov similarity with u* = κU/F_m(ζ) and θ* = κΔθ/F_h(ζ) (see _turbulent_heat_flux for F_m and F_h, which evaluate Ψ_m at z_w/L and Ψ_h at z_T/L) gives z_w/L = Ri·F_m(ζ)²/F_h(ζ). This function solves that relation for ζ by MO_NEWTON_STEPS Newton steps on ln|ζ|,

julia
R(s) = s + ln F_h − 2 ln F_m − ln|Ri| = 0,    s = ln|ζ|,
dR/ds = 1 + (φ_h(ζ z_T/z_w) − φ_h(ζ zT/z_w))/F_h − 2(φ_m(ζ) − φ_m(ζ z0/z_w))/F_m,

starting from the neutral estimate ζ₀ = Ri·ln(z_w/z0)²/ln(z_T/zT). The number of steps is fixed and there is no convergence test, so ζ is a smooth function of Ri with the sign of Ri, zero at Ri = 0, on both sides of neutral. Against the converged solution the transfer coefficient 1/(F_m F_h) agrees to 1e-12 for -100 < ζ < 1 and to 5e-5 for any ζ reached with |Ri| ≤ 10^4, over z0 from 1e-5 to 1e-2 m, zT/z0 from 0.01 to 1, and wind and temperature heights of 2 and 10 m in any combination.

The unstable result is bounded below by ZETA_UNSTABLE_MIN.

The stable functions are Beljaars & Holtslag (1991); the unstable ones Paulson (1970) with Högström's (1988) coefficients (see stability_psi_m).

julia
stability_psi_m(ζ), stability_psi_h(ζ)

  • Stable (ζ > 0): Beljaars & Holtslag (1991) eqs. 28 and 32 in closed form, with a = 1, b = 2/3, c = 5, d = 0.35: Ψ_m = −[aζ + b(ζ − c/d)e^{−dζ} + bc/d] and Ψ_h = −[(1 + 2aζ/3)^{3/2} + b(ζ − c/d)e^{−dζ} + bc/d − 1].

julia

GEMB._ground_turbulent_heat_flux Method
julia
_ground_turbulent_heat_flux(T1, sfc, cfs, β)
    -> (heat_flux_sensible, heat_flux_latent, latent_heat, dshf, dlhf)

Turbulent fluxes [W m-2, positive into the surface] of a snow-free ground surface at T1 [K] and their slopes at fixed wind, from the stability table of sfc, with the latent flux scaled by the bare-soil evaporation factor β.

The wind in the transfer law is U_e = √(U² + w*²), with U the forcing wind (floored as in sfc) and w* = (z_i·g·Q_0v/T_a)^{1/3} the free-convection velocity scale of a heated surface, Q_0v = −H/(ρ_a c_p) − 0.61·T_a·LE/(ρ_a L) the upward kinematic virtual-temperature flux and z_i = FREE_CONVECTION_HEIGHT (Beljaars, 1995; ECMWF, 2018, eqs. 3.17-3.18). Without it the transfer coefficient of a calm hour vanishes with U, and sunlit dry soil has no turbulent loss at all. w* depends on the fluxes it sets, so it is found by fixed-point iteration from w* = 1 m s-1; on a stable or neutral surface it is zero and U_e = U. A zero transfer factor sfc.C is a surface sealed from the air: every flux and slope is zero.

GEMB._thermal_solve_ground! Method
julia
_thermal_solve_ground!(temperature, dz, density, water, gn, shortwave_flux, sfc,
                       emissivity, emissivity_melt_switch, β, cfs, mp)
    -> (longwave_upward, heat_flux_sensible, heat_flux_latent, heat_flux_basal,
        evaporation_condensation, evaporation_condensation_liquid, heat_flux_ground, refrozen)

Advance the snow-and-ground column over one forcing step with the explicit finite-volume enthalpy scheme of _thermal_solve!(::ExplicitThermal, ...): one flux per face, so the column total is conserved exactly; harmonic-mean face conductances, which join the snow and the soil at their interface as Crocus joins its snow base to ISBA's first soil layer (Lafaysse et al., 2026, eqs. 95-96); a surface flux linearized in the surface cell's temperature change; and the deepest ground node a Dirichlet reservoir whose enthalpy is left unchanged.

temperature, dz, density and water are the ns snow cells (possibly none), updated in place. gn holds the ground nodes, whose enthalpies gn.H are advanced in place. shortwave_flux [W m-2] covers all ns + ng cells. The surface cell is the top snow cell, whose fluxes are the snow surface balance of _surface_fluxes_and_slopes, or with no snow cell the top ground node, whose fluxes are _ground_surface_fluxes_and_slopes with evaporation factor β.

Snow cells use GEMB's enthalpy maps, including the freezing plateau of a wet cell (_wet_cell_temperature) and _wet_cell_handoff! at the end. Ground nodes use ground_state, whose plateau freezes and thaws their water at 273.15 K.

The flux across the snow–ground face takes the snow side at min(T, 273.15). A snow cell's temperature above the melting point is energy that calculate_melt turns into melt after the solve; conducting it into the soil instead would heat the ground from snow at 0 °C. Crocus evaluates its basal flux the same way (snowcro.F90, "Lower boundary flux": PGBAS with MIN(XTT, ZSNOWTEMP)).

heat_flux_ground [W m-2, positive upward] is the step mean of the conductive flux from the top ground node into the snow cell above it; with no snow cell it is minus the energy the surface balance and shortwave deliver to the ground surface. Shortwave transmitted through the snow and absorbed by the ground is not part of it. refrozen [kg m-2] is the snow pore water frozen by _wet_cell_handoff!.

The sub-step is the explicit stability limit of the combined column, a ground node's sensible heat capacity taken with its water frozen (the smaller of the two), and the surface relaxation bound of _surface_relaxation_dt.

  • Lafaysse, M., et al. (2026). Version 3.0.2 of the Crocus snowpack model. Geosci. Model Dev. 19, 6273-6334.

GEMB._ground_vapor_exchange! Method
julia
_ground_vapor_exchange!(cols, EC, EC_liquid, mp) -> (E, EC_ground)

Exchange the step's vapor mass EC [kg m-2, positive for deposition], of which EC_liquid at LV, with the snow of cols, as expose_vapor_exchange_layer! and apply_vapor_exchange! do for a glacier column, and return the enthalpy E [J m-2] the exchanged mass carried into the snow and the part EC_ground [kg m-2] exchanged with the ground.

  • With no snow all of it is exchanged with the ground: EC_ground = EC.

  • A loss the snow cannot supply, once merged into one cell, takes that whole cell (its mass leaves at its enthalpy), and the rest, EC_ground, is exchanged with the ground.

Vapor exchanged with the ground changes neither the column's mass nor its enthalpy beyond the latent heat flux the surface balance already charged: the soil water is held fixed, so evaporation from the ground stands for water the soil resupplies, as Crocus's PEVAPCOR takes a thin snow cover's excess sublimation from the soil (snowcro.F90, SNOWCROGONE).

GEMB.gemb_core_ground Method
julia
gemb_core_ground(snow, temperature_ground, water_ground, g, cfs, mp, verbose;
                 thermal_workspace=ThermalWorkspace(), column_workspace=ColumnWorkspace())
    -> (snow, temperature_ground, water_ground, flux)

One time step of the snow over the soil column g (lower_boundary = :ground). snow is the column_state of the snow cells, possibly empty; temperature_ground [K] and water_ground (unfrozen pore water [kg m-2]) the ground cells. Returns the new snow bundle, the ground state and the step's flux terms.

The sequence is gemb_core's, applied to the snow, with the ground in the thermal solve:

  1. grain metamorphism of the snow;

  2. albedo by calculate_albedo_ground: snow-free ground at mp.ground_albedo, thin snow blended to it;

  3. shortwave absorbed in the snow as on a glacier, and what the snow transmits, or with no snow all of the net shortwave, absorbed in the top ground cell;

  4. the thermal solve of snow and ground together (_thermal_solve_ground!). Snow lighter than snow_layer_min_mass(dt) is merged into one cell and solved as frozen (or liquid) water of the top ground node, at that node's temperature, and separated again after the solve: the snow takes the node's temperature capped at 273.15 K and the node's frozen water first, so it stays snow while the node is on its freezing plateau. Melted snow runs off. This keeps the explicit sub-step at the scale of a snow cell of that mass;

  5. vapor exchange (_ground_vapor_exchange!);

  6. snowfall and rain. Over snow, calculate_accumulation. On snow-free ground, snowfall forms a new snow cell with fresh_snow_properties, and rain runs off at the melting point, its sensible heat above it entering the top ground cell;

  7. blowing snow, melt, percolation and refreeze in the snow. Water leaving the base of the snow runs off: the ground is impermeable to it. Energy beyond what melts the deepest snow cell enters the top ground cell;

  8. the snow's thickness bands (manage_layer_thickness with a ceiling of mp.ground_snow_cells_max cells and no fixed-temperature bottom) and densification.

The ground cells are never merged, split, densified or melted: their thickness, solids and pore water are fixed, and only their enthalpy changes, the pore water freezing and thawing at 273.15 K on the plateau of ground_state. The deepest ground cell is the Dirichlet reservoir at its initial temperature. There is no basal mass flux and no horizontal strain.

With verbose the step's mass and energy budgets are checked as in gemb_core.

_thermal_solve_prescribed!(temperature, dz, density, water, T_base, shortwave_flux, sfc,

julia
_trace_snow_on_base!(cols, T_base, mp) -> (E_change, melt, runoff, refreeze)

Trace snow (lighter than snow_layer_min_mass(dt), merged into one cell) on the prescribed base at T_base [K]. Its thermal resistance and heat capacity are negligible against the reservoir's, so it takes the base temperature:

GEMB.gemb_core_prescribed Method
julia
gemb_core_prescribed(snow, T_base, cfs, mp, verbose;
                     thermal_workspace=ThermalWorkspace(), column_workspace=ColumnWorkspace())
    -> (snow, flux)

One time step of the snow snow (column_state, possibly empty) on a fixed-temperature reservoir at T_base [K], this step's value of the temperature_ground forcing layer (lower_boundary = :prescribed). No ground column is solved: the reservoir stands for the ground surface, whose temperature is prescribed, typically from a measured near-surface soil temperature. It has no heat capacity in the model and is an argument of the step, not a cell, so its temperature changes only between steps, to the next prescribed value.

With snow, the step is that of the snow cells of gemb_core_ground, the thermal solve being _thermal_solve_prescribed!:

  1. grain metamorphism;

  2. albedo, the snow blending to mp.ground_albedo as over :ground (calculate_albedo_ground);

  3. shortwave, the part the snow transmits absorbed by the reservoir;

  4. the thermal solve, the deepest cell exchanging heat with the reservoir;

  5. to 8. vapor exchange, snowfall and rain, blowing snow, melt and percolation (meltwater leaving the base of the snow runs off), thickness bands and densification (_snow_after_thermal!).

Snow lighter than snow_layer_min_mass(dt) is not solved thermally: it takes the base temperature (_trace_snow_on_base!), and its surface balance is that of snow at that temperature capped at 273.15 K (_trace_snow_surface).

With no snow the surface is the prescribed ground: albedo mp.ground_albedo, its energy balance evaluated at T_base with mp.ground_emissivity and mp.ground_evaporation_factor (_prescribed_bare_surface), and the reservoir absorbs the net. Snowfall on it starts a new snow cell with fresh_snow_properties; rain runs off at the melting point, its sensible heat above it going to the reservoir.

heat_flux_basal [W m-2, positive upward] is the energy the reservoir supplies to the snow column over the step: the conductive flux into the deepest snow cell, less the melt energy the snow passes down (calculate_melt's basal surplus) and the sensible heat of rain on snow-free ground; with no or trace snow, minus the energy the surface delivers to the ground, plus the trace snow's enthalpy change. Shortwave the snow transmits is absorbed by the reservoir and is not part of it. heat_flux_ground is the same quantity. With verbose the step's mass and energy budgets are checked with the energy through the base counted as heat_flux_basal.
