Internals: Support ​

Non-exported constants, metadata tables, interpolation, and remaining helpers. See Internals for the caveat on depending on these.

GEMB.AQUIFER_TOLERANCE Constant
julia
AQUIFER_TOLERANCE

Diagnostic threshold, not a branch tolerance: how far above irreducible a cell must be for aquifer_diagnostics to call it saturated. Deliberately loose, and expressed as a fraction of the cell's pore space rather than as a mass.

The reason is physical, not arithmetic. calculate_melt leaves a retaining cell at exactly its irreducible water, but calculate_density then compacts the cell in the same timestep, shrinking the pore space that irreducible water was computed against. The cell is left marginally over-saturated — a genuine excess of order 1e-4 kg m-2 per timestep, which accumulates between melt events. A mass threshold cannot separate that from a thin aquifer, because the two differ in saturation, not in mass: compaction residue sits ~1e-7 of pore space above irreducible, while ponded water reaches 1e-1 to 1. Nothing in the physics reads this; it only decides what aquifer_diagnostics reports.

GEMB.CROCUS_FIRN_CALIBRATION Constant
julia
CROCUS_FIRN_CALIBRATION

The default Crocus firn handoff and its calibration factors (ModelParameters fields crocus_handoff_* and crocus_overburden_factor): :Crocus hands firn to :Arthern under :rate_match from 300 kg m-3, with the rate factors of the firn-core calibration of the GEMB v2.0 paper. The handoff law's creep activation energy, ARTHERN_ACTIVATION_ENERGY + crocus_handoff_activation_energy_shift = 50.1 kJ mol-1, is above the lowest measured value for firn creep (45 kJ mol-1) and the grain-growth energy (GRAIN_GROWTH_EG, 42.4 kJ mol-1). CROCUS_PUBLISHED_HANDOFF is the same fields at the published laws.

GEMB.DT_MIN_WARN Constant
julia
DT_MIN_WARN

Smallest stable sub-timestep the thermal solver will accept without warning [s]. Below this, the Von Neumann stability limit has collapsed, which in practice means a near-zero dz cell rather than a genuinely stiff column.

ExplicitThermal only. ImplicitThermal is unconditionally stable, never consults _max_safe_dt, and so never warns however thin a cell becomes.

GEMB.HEAT_CAPACITY_WATER Constant
julia
HEAT_CAPACITY_WATER

Specific heat capacity of liquid water [J kg-1 K-1]. Carries the sensible heat of above-freezing liquid entering the column — i.e. rain — via the two-argument specific_enthalpy_water(mp, T), under the rain_heat_capacity = :water default. Pore water in GEMB is carried strictly at the melting point, so this does not appear in the percolation or refreeze budgets; heat_capacity(mp, T) remains the ice-matrix value everywhere else. Value from the Community Firn Model (solver.py, c_liq).

GEMB.THERMAL_EXPLICIT_SAFETY_FACTOR Constant
julia
THERMAL_EXPLICIT_SAFETY_FACTOR

Fraction of the diffusive stability limit (_max_safe_dt) the explicit sub-step may use. Default of mp.thermal_explicit_safety_factor; ExplicitThermal only.

The limit is exact for the diffusion stencil at the start of the forcing step; the factor covers the change of the heat capacity and conductivity within the step. The surface energy balance needs no stability margin: it is linearized in the surface cell's new temperature, which makes its feedback stable at any sub-step. A separate bound, THERMAL_SURFACE_STEP_FRACTION of the surface relaxation time, keeps the surface flux accurate. 0.8 is inherited from the MATLAB model. Runs that want no stability limit at all should use ImplicitThermal, which is unconditionally stable and does not consult this.

GEMB.THERMAL_IMPLICIT_DT_TARGET Constant
julia
THERMAL_IMPLICIT_DT_TARGET

Target sub-step [s] for ImplicitThermal, and the ceiling on the resulting count.

Backward Euler is unconditionally stable, so this is an accuracy control, not a stability one — the distinction that makes the implicit path cheap. The count is bounded by how fast the surface forcing changes and is independent of the cell count, of dz, and of the stiffest cell in the column, unlike the explicit path's ~29 sub-steps.

Calibrated on a year of 3-hourly synthetic forcing (264-cell column, no spinup), comparing whole-model output against a finely sub-stepped implicit run at 168.75 s. RMS temperature differences [K] and the annual melt difference:

target [s] n_sub(3h) RMS surface RMS interior Δmelt runtime 5400 2 1.164 0.143 -2.46% — 2700 4 0.526 0.140 -1.66% — 1800 6 0.419 0.126 -1.19% 3.6 s 900 12 0.341 0.141 -1.03% 5.8 s 450 24 0.272 0.119 -0.08% 9.6 s

Runtimes re-measured after the static condensation of the interior rows (2.67x); the accuracy columns are unchanged by it, since it alters only how the same solve is factorized.

900 s is the knee: melt is within ~1% and the surface RMS has come down 3.4x from the two-sub-step case, while halving again costs 79% more runtime for 0.07 K.

The interior column is not converging in this table — it floors at ~0.13 K regardless of target. That floor is not solver error: manage_layer_thickness merges and splits on discrete thickness thresholds, so a single flipped merge changes the deep discretization by more than the time integration does. Whole-model differencing therefore cannot resolve implicit accuracy below ~0.13 K, which is why the surface RMS and the melt total are what this is tuned on.

GEMB.energy_tolerance Method
julia
energy_tolerance(E_reference)

Absolute tolerance [J] for a verbose-only energy-conservation check whose budget totals E_reference.

E_TOLERANCE alone is a relative 1e-14 against a deep column's ~1e11 J of enthalpy, which only ever passed because both sides of the check were bit-identical arithmetic. Working in enthalpy adds real round-trip noise (~3e-6 J per deep-cell merge), so every check is relative-with-floor: the floor keeps small columns strict, the relative term scales with the budget being checked.

GEMB.StabilityTable Type
julia
StabilityTable

Tabulated Monin-Obukhov transfer functions for one set of measurement heights (z_w, z_T) and roughness lengths (z0 for momentum, zT for heat and moisture). Built by _stability_table, evaluated by _stability_lookup.

For each sign of the bulk Richardson number Ri the table holds, on nodes uniform in q = ln|Ri|, the converged stability parameter as s = ln|ζ| and G = ln(F_m·F_h), each with its exact derivative with respect to q, for cubic Hermite interpolation.

GEMB._fit_buffer! Method
julia
_fit_buffer!(buffer, n) -> buffer

Resize buffer to exactly n, growing or shrinking.

The exception to _resize_workspace!'s grow-only rule, for a buffer standing in for an array its consumer sums, or broadcasts against the column: there a surplus tail would enter the result or raise a length mismatch, so "at least n" is not good enough. resize! keeps capacity, so this reallocates only when the column grows past its high-water mark.

source
GEMB._resize_workspace! Method
julia
_resize_workspace!(ws, n) -> ws

Grow every buffer in ws to length n, or leave it alone if it is already that long.

resize! keeps the underlying capacity, so the column length is reached on the first timestep and every later call is a no-op. The buffers hold no state between timesteps — each is fully written before it is read — so the contents after a grow are deliberately undefined.

Callers index by m/n computed from the column, never by length(buffer), so a buffer that is longer than needed (a column that shrank) is still correct.

ws is unannotated because the only thing the body requires is a _workspace_buffers method, which is also the extension point a new workspace type adds.

_fit_buffer! is the exception to grow-only: a buffer standing in for an array the caller sums, or broadcasts against the column, must be exactly the cell count or the surplus tail enters the result. resize! keeps capacity, so it reallocates only when the column grows past the high-water mark.

GEMB.dendritic_grain_radius Method
julia
dendritic_grain_radius(dendricity, sphericity) -> r [mm]

Effective grain radius of dendritic snow diagnosed from its dendricity and sphericity, Brun et al. (1992) as GEMB carries it: grain size (diameter) is not independent state while dendricity > 0, so any scheme that changes (δ, S) must re-derive the radius.

Two inline copies of this expression predate the helper — calculate_grain_size (the dendritic branch), and the wind-dependent fresh-snow radius in calculate_accumulation. They are left in place because they floor at GDN_TOLERANCE on the radius where this floors at GDN_TOLERANCE*2 on the diameter, and the two differ in the degenerate branch only; unifying them is not bit-identical. New callers should use this.

References

  • Brun, E., David, P., Sudul, M. and Brunot, G. (1992). A numerical model to simulate snow-cover stratigraphy for operational avalanche forecasting. J. Glaciol. 38, 13-22.
source
GEMB.independent_grain_radius Method
julia
independent_grain_radius(mp) -> Bool

Whether the grain radius of dendritic snow is state of its own rather than dendritic_grain_radius of its dendricity and sphericity: true under grain_growth_method = :FlannerZender2006, whose SNICAR aging carries one prognostic radius from fresh snow on (see calculate_grain_size). Where this holds, mixing and drift leave the radius to mix_grains's non-dendritic rule or unchanged instead of re-deriving it.

GEMB.saturation_vapor_pressure_ice Method
julia
saturation_vapor_pressure_ice(T) -> e_s [Pa]

Saturation vapor pressure over ice, Murphy & Koop (2005) eq. 7, with T in kelvin:

julia
ln(e_s) = 9.550426 − 5723.265/T + 3.53068·ln(T) − 0.00728332·T

valid above 110 K; 611.657 Pa at the triple point.

Shared by the latent-heat flux in turbulent_heat_flux (evaluated at the surface temperature, for a sub-freezing surface) and by blowing_snow_sublimation_rate (evaluated at the air temperature). Companion of saturation_vapor_pressure_water, which the flux uses at the melting point.

References

  • Murphy, D. M. and Koop, T. (2005). Review of the vapor pressures of ice and supercooled water for atmospheric applications. Q. J. R. Meteorol. Soc. 131, 1539-1565. Eq. 7.

GEMB.saturation_vapor_pressure_water Method
julia
saturation_vapor_pressure_water(T) -> e_s [Pa]

Saturation vapor pressure over liquid water, supercooled below the triple point, Murphy & Koop (2005) eq. 10 (p. 1552), with T in kelvin:

julia
ln(e_s) = 54.842763 − 6763.22/T − 4.210·ln(T) + 0.000367·T
          + tanh(0.0415·(T − 218.8))·(53.878 − 1331.22/T − 9.44523·ln(T) + 0.014025·T)

valid for 123 < T < 332 K; outside that range it throws a DomainError. Its Table C1 (p. 1561) gives 611.213 Pa at 273.15 K and 611.657 Pa at the triple point, where it meets saturation_vapor_pressure_ice (eq. 7 of the same paper); below the triple point it lies above the ice curve.

The latent-heat flux (turbulent_heat_flux) uses it only for a surface at the melting point, where the exchanging phase is liquid water; every colder surface exchanges vapor with ice.

References

  • Murphy, D. M. and Koop, T. (2005). Review of the vapor pressures of ice and supercooled water for atmospheric applications. Q. J. R. Meteorol. Soc. 131, 1539-1565. Eq. 10.
source
GEMB.saturation_vapor_pressure_water_derivative Method
julia
saturation_vapor_pressure_water_derivative(T) -> de_s/dT [Pa K-1]

Temperature derivative of saturation_vapor_pressure_water, the analytic derivative of Murphy & Koop (2005) eq. 10: with s = 0.0415·(T − 218.8) and g(T) = 53.878 − 1331.22/T − 9.44523·ln(T) + 0.014025·T,

julia
de_s/dT = e_s·(6763.22/T² − 4.210/T + 0.000367 + 0.0415·sech²(s)·g(T)
               + tanh(s)·(1331.22/T² − 9.44523/T + 0.014025))

Valid over the same 123 < T < 332 K, and throws the same DomainError outside it.

GEMB.specific_humidity Method
julia
specific_humidity(vapor_pressure, pressure_air) -> q [kg kg-1]

Specific humidity from vapour and total pressure, q = εe/(P - (1-ε)e) with ε = MOLAR_MASS_RATIO_VAPOR_AIR.

Used by blowing_snow_sublimation_rate, which needs q rather than e because the Gordon et al. (2006) sublimation rate is written against the saturation specific humidity. The turbulent latent-heat flux works in vapour pressure directly and does not call this.

References

  • Gordon, M., Simon, K. and Taylor, P. A. (2006). On snow depth predictions with the Canadian land surface scheme including a parametrization of blowing snow sublimation. Atmos.-Ocean 44, 239-255.

GEMB.surface_roughness Method
julia
surface_roughness(mp, density_surface, water_surface) -> z0 [m]

Aerodynamic roughness length of the surface, after Bougamont et al. (2005): dry snow, wet snow, and bare ice each get their own value (Z0_SNOW_DRY, Z0_SNOW_WET, Z0_ICE), with "ice" meaning a surface cell at mp.density_ice and "wet" meaning one holding liquid water.

Read by the turbulent fluxes in calculate_temperature and by the 5 m gust speed in drift_wind_speed, so both see the same surface.

References

  • Bougamont, M., Bamber, J. L. and Greuell, W. (2005). A surface mass balance model for the Greenland Ice Sheet. J. Geophys. Res. 110, F04018.
